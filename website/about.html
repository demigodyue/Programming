About Nepal

A landlocked country of 30 million people between India and China, holding the highest ground on Earth and one of the densest concentrations of living culture anywhere.

The Himalaya

Peaks of Nepal

Eight of the world's ten tallest mountains stand in Nepal — Everest, Kanchenjunga, Lhotse, Makalu, Cho Oyu, Dhaulagiri, Manaslu and Annapurna I. Each one carries a breathtaking, mesmerizing view and a landscape entirely its own.

Everest was first summited on 29 May 1953 by Tenzing Norgay Sherpa and Edmund Hillary. Annapurna I was the first 8,000 m peak ever climbed, in 1950 — and remains the deadliest.

Culture of Nepal

The culture of Nepal encompasses the many cultures belonging to the 125 distinct ethnic groups living within its borders. It is expressed through music and dance, art and craft, folklore, languages and literature, philosophy and religion, festivals and celebration, and food and drink.

Nepal is also the only country with a living goddess. The Kumari is a young girl chosen from the Newar Shakya community, worshipped as an incarnation of the goddess Taleju, and carried through Kathmandu on a wooden chariot each Indra Jatra.

Birthplace

The land of Lord Buddha

Siddhartha Gautama was born in Lumbini, in the Terai plains of southern Nepal, around 563 BCE. Hinduism and Buddhism have shared the same courtyards here for centuries — the same shrine is often venerated by both, and no one finds that strange.

Boudhanath, the great white stupa on the eastern edge of Kathmandu, is one of the largest in the world. Every morning pilgrims walk clockwise around it, spinning prayer wheels beneath a canopy of coloured flags.

On the Table

Nepali Cuisine

Rich, spiced and unfussy — built on rice, lentils and whatever the hillside gives, then lifted with turmeric, cumin, ginger and timur pepper.

Momo

The national obsession. Steamed or fried dumplings filled with buff, chicken or vegetables, served with a fiery tomato-sesame achar. Sold everywhere from bus parks to hotel kitchens.

Chowmein

Tibetan-Chinese noodles adopted wholesale and made Nepali — wok-fried hard and fast with cabbage, carrot, spring onion and a heavy hand of chilli.

Khir

Rice slowly cooked down in milk with cardamom, sugar and almonds. Made for festivals, birthdays and Shrawan Sankranti — the day Nepal eats khir by tradition.

Panipuri

Hollow crisp shells filled with potato and chickpea, then dunked in cold spiced tamarind water. Street food at its best — eaten standing up, one at a time.

Crimson & Blue

The only flag that isn't a rectangle

Every other national flag in the world is a quadrilateral. Nepal's is two stacked pennants — and its exact geometry is written into the constitution as a set of construction instructions.

The crimson is the colour of the rhododendron, the national flower, and of courage. The blue border stands for peace. The moon on the upper pennant represents the cool of the Himalaya and the royal house; the sun below represents the heat of the lowlands. Together they carry the wish that Nepal will last as long as the sun and moon themselves.

This site borrows its own palette from somewhere else on the flag — the warm terracotta and gold of a Himalayan sunrise.
