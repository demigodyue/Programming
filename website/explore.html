Explore Nepal

Snow-capped mountains, terraced hillsides and the timeless charm of villages and marketplaces. Every corner of Nepal tells a story — here is where to find them.

The Lay of the Land

Three countries in one

Nepal is only 800 km long, yet it climbs from 60 m in the Terai plains to 8,849 m at the summit of Everest. That rise happens in under 200 km, which is why you can start the morning among rhinos and elephants and end the week walking on a glacier.

The country divides neatly into three bands: the Himalaya in the north, the hills and valleys through the middle, and the subtropical Terai along the southern border.

Six Places

Where to Go

Between them these cover the whole spread of the country, from glacier to temple courtyard.

Himalaya

The Khumbu

Sagarmatha National Park, Sherpa villages at 4,000 m, and the mountain Nepal calls Sagarmatha — "forehead of the sky". The classic trailhead is Lukla.

Trek Here
Lakes

Gokyo Valley

A chain of six glacial lakes at 4,700–5,000 m, fed by the Ngozumpa — the longest glacier in the Himalaya. The highest freshwater lake system in the world.

Trek Here
Trails

Ama Dablam

Prayer-flag ridges, stone chortens and yak caravans, with the fluted pyramid of Ama Dablam filling the sky at the head of the valley.

High camp

Base Camps

Spring turns the moraine into a tented town. Yaks haul the loads, kitchens run on kerosene, and climbers wait weeks for a weather window.

Valley

Kathmandu Valley

One of the largest stupas in the world, ringed by monasteries and butter-lamp smoke. Add Patan and Bhaktapur for Newari brick, wood and bronze at its finest.

Festivals

Nepal keeps a living goddess. During Indra Jatra the Kumari is drawn through Kathmandu on a wooden chariot while masked dancers clear the way.

On the Plate

Eat your way across the map

Food changes with altitude. In the Terai you get panipuri and sweet khir at the roadside; in the valley towns it's Newari feasts and steamed momo; up in the Himalaya it's thukpa, tsampa and butter tea, because that is what grows and what keeps you warm.
