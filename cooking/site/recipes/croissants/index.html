Butter Croissants

Shatteringly flaky, honeycombed croissants made from a yeasted dough laminated with cold butter over three folds.

Method

  1. 1

    Step 1: Mix the flour, sugar, salt and yeast. Add the water, milk and soft butter and knead for 5 minutes until just smooth. Flatten into a rectangle, wrap and refrigerate overnight (8–12 hours).

  2. 2

    Step 2: Pound the cold butter between sheets of parchment into a 17 cm square. Chill until firm but still pliable — it should bend without cracking.

  3. 3

    Step 3: Roll the dough into a 34 × 17 cm rectangle. Set the butter on one half, fold the other half over and pinch the edges to seal it in.

  4. 4

    Step 4: Roll into a 20 × 60 cm strip and fold it in three like a letter. Wrap and chill for 30 minutes. Repeat the rolling, folding and chilling twice more, for three folds in total.

  5. 5

    Step 5: Roll the dough into a 25 × 65 cm rectangle about 4 mm thick. Trim the edges and cut long triangles with 10 cm bases.

  6. 6

    Step 6: Cut a 1 cm notch in the centre of each base, gently stretch the tip, then roll up from base to tip. Set on lined trays with the tip tucked underneath.

  7. 7

    Step 7: Proof at 24–26 °C for 2–2½ hours until puffy, wobbly and the layers are visible. Any warmer and the butter will melt out.

  8. 8

    Step 8: Heat the oven to 200 °C. Brush with egg wash and bake for 15–18 minutes until deep golden. Cool on a rack for 15 minutes.

The story of this dish

The croissant descends from the Austrian kipferl, a crescent-shaped bread popularised in Paris around 1839 by August Zang's Viennese bakery. French bakers later swapped the dense dough for laminated, yeasted pastry, and by the early 1900s the flaky croissant had become a Parisian breakfast fixture. The tale of it celebrating a victory over the Ottomans is a legend.
