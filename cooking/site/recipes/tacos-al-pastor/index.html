Tacos al PastorTacos al Pastor

Pork marinated in guajillo, ancho and achiote, seared until charred and tucked into warm corn tortillas with grilled pineapple, onion and cilantro.

Method

  1. 1

    Step 1: Toast the chilies in a dry skillet for 20–30 seconds per side until fragrant, then soak in hot water for 15 minutes.

  2. 2

    Step 2: Drain the chilies and blend with the pineapple juice, vinegar, garlic, achiote, spices and salt until very smooth.

  3. 3

    Step 3: Coat the pork slices in the marinade, cover and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, or overnight.

  4. 4

    Step 4: Sear the pork in batches on a very hot grill or cast-iron pan for 3–4 minutes per side until charred at the edges. (For a trompo effect, stack the slices tightly on a skewer and roast at 200 °C for about an hour.)

  5. 5

    Step 5: Grill the pineapple slices for 2–3 minutes per side until caramelised.

  6. 6

    Step 6: Chop the pork and pineapple into small pieces. Warm the tortillas on a dry comal or pan.

  7. 7

    Step 7: Fill doubled-up tortillas with pork and pineapple, top with onion and cilantro and serve with lime and salsa verde.

The story of this dish

Al pastor — “shepherd style” — descends from the shawarma brought to Puebla and Mexico City by Lebanese immigrants in the early twentieth century. Mexican cooks swapped lamb for pork, added a chili-achiote marinade and crowned the vertical spit, the trompo, with a pineapple. A good taquero slices meat and a sliver of pineapple straight onto the tortilla.
