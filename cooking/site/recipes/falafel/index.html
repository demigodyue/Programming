Falafel with TahiniFalafel with Tahini

Herb-packed falafel made the proper way from soaked dried chickpeas — crisp outside, bright green inside — with lemony tahini sauce.

Method

  1. 1

    Step 1: Cover the chickpeas with plenty of cold water and soak for 12–24 hours. They will more than double in size. Drain and pat dry.

  2. 2

    Step 2: Pulse the chickpeas, onion, garlic, herbs and chili in a food processor, scraping down the sides, until the mixture looks like coarse sand. Stop before it becomes a paste.

  3. 3

    Step 3: Add the cumin, coriander, cayenne and salt and pulse to combine. Chill for 30 minutes.

  4. 4

    Step 4: Whisk the tahini, lemon juice, garlic and salt — it will seize and thicken — then whisk in the cold water a little at a time until smooth and pourable.

  5. 5

    Step 5: Heat the oil to 175 °C. Just before frying, stir the baking soda and sesame seeds into the falafel mixture.

  6. 6

    Step 6: Shape heaped tablespoons into balls or small patties without compacting them too hard. Fry in batches for 3–4 minutes until deep golden-brown, then drain on a rack.

  7. 7

    Step 7: Serve hot in pita with tomato, cucumber, pickles and plenty of tahini sauce.

The story of this dish

Falafel is widely believed to come from Egypt, where Coptic Christians made ta'ameya from fava beans as a meatless Lenten meal. As it spread across the Levant, cooks switched to chickpeas, and it is now a street-food staple from Cairo to Beirut and Jerusalem.
