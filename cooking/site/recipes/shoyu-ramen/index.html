Shoyu RamenShoyu Ramen

Tokyo-style ramen in a clear chicken-dashi broth seasoned with soy tare, topped with braised pork belly and jammy marinated eggs.

Method

  1. 1

    Step 1: Sear the tied pork belly on all sides in a heavy pot over medium-high heat until well browned, about 8 minutes.

  2. 2

    Step 2: Add the soy sauce, sake, mirin, sugar, water, green onions and ginger. Bring to a simmer, cover with the lid slightly ajar and braise on low for 2 hours, turning the pork every 30 minutes, until a skewer slides in easily.

  3. 3

    Step 3: While the pork braises, boil the eggs for 6½ minutes, chill them in iced water and peel. Strain 240 ml of the warm braising liquid over the eggs in a small container and marinate for at least 4 hours — ideally make the eggs and pork the day before.

  4. 4

    Step 4: For the broth, warm the chicken stock with the kombu over low heat for 10 minutes without letting it boil, then remove the kombu. Add the garlic and ginger and simmer gently for 20 minutes.

  5. 5

    Step 5: Turn off the heat, add the bonito flakes and steep for 5 minutes. Strain the broth and keep it hot.

  6. 6

    Step 6: For the tare, simmer the soy sauce, mirin and sake in a small pan for 1 minute.

  7. 7

    Step 7: Slice the chashu 5 mm thick. Cook the noodles in plenty of boiling unsalted water according to the packet, usually 1–2 minutes, and drain well.

  8. 8

    Step 8: Put 2 tablespoons of tare in each warm bowl, add about 400 ml hot broth and stir. Add the noodles, folding them so they sit neatly, then top with chashu, a halved egg, menma, nori, green onions and a few drops of sesame oil.

The story of this dish

Ramen reached Japan with Chinese immigrants in the late 1800s, sold as “shina soba” from stalls in Yokohama's Chinatown. After the Second World War, cheap wheat and Momofuku Ando's 1958 instant noodles turned it into a national obsession. Shoyu ramen, seasoned with soy sauce, is the classic Tokyo style and one of the oldest.
